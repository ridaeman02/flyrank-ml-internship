Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring & Ranking

By FlyRank ML Intern • Track: Applied Search Intelligence • Repo: github.com/ridaeman02/flyrank-ml-internship

Abstract

Content decay is a critical challenge for online publishers, where high-performing pages gradually lose organic search visibility over time. This project develops an evidence-backed Machine Learning pipeline to identify and rank declining content pages for editorial refresh prioritization. Using a 9.8M-row daily performance snapshot from the FlyRank warehouse release (March 2026), we engineered client-normalized search signals and evaluated models on a strict client-holdout split (GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id). Our Logistic Regression model achieved a 0.5000 Precision@50 score, representing a 19% relative lift over standard heuristic baseline rules (0.4200 Precision@50) and significantly outperforming the base test set rate (0.2890). The resulting system produces transparent action queues with human-interpretable reason codes to optimize editor review capacity.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Managing large content portfolios across dozens of client domains presents a capacity bottleneck: editorial teams cannot manually inspect thousands of pages every week. Simple heuristic rules (such as "refresh any page older than 6 months") generate massive lists of false alarms, wasting valuable writer hours on healthy pages while missing critical traffic decay.

This research supports the decision of which declining pages to review and refresh first. By framing content refresh as a ranking problem, we predict content decay probabilities and construct a prioritized queue sorted by impact opportunity.

2. Dataset & Safety

This study utilizes the FlyRank Pseudonymized Warehouse Release (v20260703) hosted on Hugging Face. We queried daily performance facts for March 2026 (9,841,378 rows across 70 clients) aggregated at the page level.

  • Tables Used: dim_content (page creation dates and metadata) and fact_content_daily_performance (daily impressions, clicks, average position).
  • Public Safety: All client, content, keyword, and URL identifiers are pseudonymized. No raw names, domains, or unmasked search queries appear anywhere in this work.
  • Deliberate Exclusions: Product-derived rule flags (such as health_score or priority_score) were excluded to prevent circular reasoning.

3. Methodology & Split Design

We partitioned March 2026 into a 15-day feature window (March 1–15) and a 16-day outcome window (March 16–31).

Label Definition: A page is labeled as declining (is_declining = 1) if its outcome-window impressions drop below 80% of its feature-window impressions.

Validation Design: We used GroupShuffleSplit grouped on client_hash_id (75% train, 25% test). Holding out entire clients ensures that the model is tested on unseen websites, preventing scale and domain leakage.

Feature Prep: Impressions and clicks were standardized within each client (imp_norm, clk_norm) to allow model parameters to generalize across websites of different sizes.

4. Results & Baseline Comparison

We compared Logistic Regression and Random Forest models against a heuristic baseline rule (pages older than 180 days ranking on page 1 or 2). All methods were evaluated on the same client-holdout split using ROC-AUC and Precision@50.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Observational Data: This research measures historical correlation. It does not claim causal proof that refreshing a page guarantees a specific traffic increase.
  • Short Outcome Windows: A 15-day outcome window includes natural traffic volatility, which introduces noise into binary decline labels.
  • Unbalanced Client History: Client tracking start dates vary across the warehouse dataset.
